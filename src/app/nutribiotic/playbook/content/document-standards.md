# Document Standards

The house standard for any document built for this department: sales sheets,
order guides, offer sheets, the product guide, talking points, whatever
comes next. Read this before drafting one, and check a draft against it
before it ships. Raised while building the med spa body-care guide,
2026-08-13, first direction set; add to this file as more comes in.

## Cut prose, lead with facts

The instinct is: state the fact, don't cushion it. A caveat, a disclaimer,
or a section-note paragraph that just restates the table below it reads as
padding to a buyer scanning in seconds, not as care.

- Card and section copy: bolded fact-fragments over full explanatory
  sentences. &ldquo;0 sulfates, 0 parabens.&rdquo; beats &ldquo;This formula
  contains no sulfates or parabens.&rdquo;
- Hero claim lists: 2&ndash;3 sharpest points in large bold single-column
  type, not 6 items in a dense 2-column grid.
- Hedges get cut once the number behind them is real. A pump-yield estimate
  didn't need &ldquo;not a published figure, confirm with your
  equipment&rdquo; once the real spec was in hand; a formulation fact didn't
  need &ldquo;not medical claims&rdquo; stapled to it. Trust your own
  operational numbers (equipment specs, a phone number) as ground truth,
  not something that needs a disclaimer.
- One contact line per page, repeated identically: his personal cell, not
  the company 800 number, email, or website.

## Frame pricing to favor the reader

Same math, presented so it reads as generous rather than neutral
accounting. The audience is a partner deciding whether to stock the line.

- &ldquo;Markup&rdquo; not &ldquo;Margin&rdquo;: 100% markup and 50% margin
  are the identical 2x ratio; markup is the bigger, more generous-reading
  number.
- &ldquo;Minimum retail &ge; $X&rdquo; not &ldquo;Suggested retail
  $X&rdquo;: reframes the wholesale price as a floor the partner can beat,
  not a ceiling they're capped at.
- The payoff column (Profit) goes far right, with a subtle highlight. That's
  the number that closes the sale, so it reads as the table's takeaway, not
  a buried column.
- Say what NutriBiotic &ldquo;suggests&rdquo; (the higher number, e.g. 3x)
  separately from what it &ldquo;asks a minimum&rdquo; of (the floor, e.g.
  2x). The floor is presented as a partner-protective guarantee, not a
  suggestion the partner might undercut.

## Typography, checked every time

Sans by default, serif (Fraunces) for display titles only. This is an
agency-wide rule, not new to this department, but it's easy to let it leak:
product names, prices, and stat numbers are body content, not display text,
even when they're the largest thing on a card. Caught 2026-08-13 auditing a
finished file against this exact rule, after a typography question surfaced
it: Fraunces had leaked onto product names, prices, and two stat numbers.
Fixed on the spot. Re-check this specifically before calling a document
done, it's the kind of thing that passes a glance and fails a close look.
