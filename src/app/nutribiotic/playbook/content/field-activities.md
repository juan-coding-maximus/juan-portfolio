# Field Activities by Revenue · proposed

Status: **proposed, not yet approved.** The order and the method changes below are a proposal. Until
I approve them, nothing here changes the Sales Playbook, and every rule there still stands.

Every job I do in the field, ranked by how much it moves revenue or saves time, and sorted into one
of three piles: **focus** (do more of it, it pays the most), **improve the method** (worth doing,
done the wrong way today), or **leave alone** (already fine, don't spend thought on it).

A fourth pile, **test with the budget**, holds paid channels whose return reads straight off a dashboard as ROAS. NutriBiotic has a budget for them.

Three levers decide the rank:

1. **Better clients.** Time spent on accounts that can buy more.
2. **More often.** More touches on the accounts that already buy.
3. **Better seller.** Better qualified leads and a higher close rate.

Efficiency counts too, but only as time handed back to the three levers.

## What the data says (measured 2026-10-02)

Every number is from the OS, my book only (owner 36242368). Touch logging began 2026-07-20.

- **Revenue sits in the top grades.** Grades A, B and C are 174 of 552 accounts and $27,222 of
  $35,117 in the last 18 months, 77 percent. Grade A alone is 17 accounts and 32 percent.
- **Most of those top accounts have stopped buying.** Only 15 of the 174 A, B and C accounts ordered
  in the last 18 months.
- **Frequency is the only signal so far.** Of my 37 active accounts, the 13 with 3 or more touches
  since 2026-07-04 placed all 3 orders the group placed ($928.83). The 24 with fewer placed none.
  16 had no touch at all.
- **Follow-up barely happens.** 4 of 205 in-person touches got a follow-up email within 2 days, 7
  within 7 days. 30 drafted emails sit unsent; 18 have gone out.
- **Field days run half full.** Median 5 visits per field day across 39 days (216 visits), against a
  10-stop plan. 15.5 miles per visit.
- **New doors are slow and rare.** 1 prospect placed a first order since logging began ($178.44,
  3 touches, 15 days). Of 34 prospects landed from a Places search, 1 has been touched.
- **Order size is flat.** Average order $237 to $308 a quarter, 7.1 to 7.6 lines, no upward trend
  2024 to mid-2026.

**The gap that limits all of it:** the ERP orders on file end 2026-06-30, before logging began. Until
a fresh order export lands, no activity's effect on orders can be measured, so this ranking rests on
the book's shape and my own judgment, not on proven touch-to-order rates.

## Each activity

### 1. Appointment setting with decision makers · focus
- **What it is.** Booking a set time with the buyer by phone or email, then running the whole visit
  inside that appointment.
- **How it moves revenue.** It buys the decision maker's attention, which a walk-in rarely gets. It is
  how Plameca's own reps book meetings: call ahead, then a reminder email (Sales Playbook section 10).
- **How I prioritize it.** Grade A and B first, 91 accounts, of which only 10 ordered in 18 months.
- **How I improve it.** One call script and one request email, approved once and reused. Mark every
  visit as scheduled or walk-in, so the close rate of each becomes measurable.
- **Evidence.** None yet: nothing in the OS marks a visit as scheduled. Of 184 scheduled SDR rows, 24
  are done. Measure to add: a scheduled flag on the activity.

### 2. Restock touches on active accounts · focus
- **What it is.** Calling or visiting the accounts that already buy, to see what needs restocking. In
  person I walk the shelf with them; a call saves the drive.
- **How it moves revenue.** It is the only lever with a signal so far: the 13 active accounts with 3
  or more touches placed every order the group placed.
- **How I prioritize it.** Accounts past their own reorder gap first (the territory median is 41
  days, measured 2026-08-02). Walk the shelf when a route already passes; call when it doesn't.
- **How I improve it.** A call sheet built on reorder cadence, not on stale pipeline steps, which is
  what the app's "overdue" means today. Target: 3 or more touches a quarter on every active account.
- **Evidence.** 16 of 37 active accounts had no touch since 2026-07-04. Calls to active accounts: 41,
  one followed by an order within 14 days ($282).

### 3. Reactivating A, B and C accounts that stopped · focus
- **What it is.** Working the high-grade accounts with no order in 18 months back to a first reorder.
- **How it moves revenue.** 159 of the 174 A, B and C accounts have not ordered in 18 months, and
  those grades hold 77 percent of the book's recent revenue. This is where seeing better clients
  pays.
- **How I prioritize it.** Grade A first, worked in route clusters, never alphabetically. Open with
  "what changed" before any pitch (Sales Playbook section 3).
- **How I improve it.** Set the number of touches before an account is parked with a dated note. I
  propose 3, which fills the open TODO in Sales Playbook section 6.
- **Evidence.** Dormant accounts had no order follow any touch since logging began. Measure: first
  reorder per reactivated account, once fresh ERP orders land.

### 4. Follow-up within two days · improve the method
- **What it is.** The email after a visit: what we discussed, what they asked for, the next step.
- **How it moves revenue.** A good email sent on time keeps the visit's momentum and closes the open
  ask.
- **How I prioritize it.** Every in-person visit, within two days.
- **How I improve it.** The drafts already exist; sending is what doesn't happen. A protected 30
  minutes at the start of the 4pm email block on each field day, working the Outbound queue to empty.
  Email templates and the drafter are the key tooling here.
- **Evidence.** 4 of 205 in-person touches followed up within 2 days. 30 drafts pending, 18 sent, 22
  dismissed. No order signal yet either way.

### 5. Week and route planning · improve the method
- **What it is.** Choosing who I see, which day, in what order.
- **How it moves revenue.** It multiplies everything above: more visits a day, less uncertainty
  between them.
- **How I prioritize it.** Appointments anchor the week; restock and reactivation stops fill around
  them; prospects fill what is left.
- **How I improve it.** One clear picture of the optimal week, not only the day. Mark each planned
  stop done or skipped, so plan against actual is visible.
- **Evidence.** Median 5 visits per field day against a 10-stop plan; 15.5 miles per visit. All 51
  planned stops on file still read "planned".

### 6. Placing orders, with the upsell · improve the method
- **What it is.** Taking a stated need and sending it to orders@nutribiotic.com, plus the client's
  confirmation.
- **How it moves revenue.** Each order is a chance to add lines and reach a discount break.
- **How I prioritize it.** Same day, every time.
- **How I improve it.** The order draft names the next volume break (10, 15, 20 percent at 48, 96, 144
  units of one SKU) or the new-SKU promotion (25, 30, 50 percent at 1, 10, 20 new SKUs), and any top
  seller they don't carry. Nothing computes either today.
- **Evidence.** Average order $237 to $308 and 7.1 to 7.6 lines, flat since 2024.

### 7. First visit: discovery and sample · improve the method
- **What it is.** The discovery conversation at a new door, and a sample left behind.
- **How it moves revenue.** It is where qualification happens: a well-qualified first visit decides
  whether the account is worth a second.
- **How I prioritize it.** Prospects scored 50 or higher get a visit; under 50 get a call or email.
- **How I improve it.** Log every sample dropped, and put a follow-up call 7 days later onto a route.
  Run the golden minute from Sales Playbook section 10.
- **Evidence.** 1 new account since logging began: 3 touches, 15 days to first order. Sample drops
  are not recorded anywhere yet.

### 8. Prospect search and scoring near A, B and C anchors · improve the method
- **What it is.** Finding stores and clinics not yet in the book, scoring them, and placing them on
  the map.
- **How it moves revenue.** The book does not yet hold every potential client. New revenue, even
  small, beats zero.
- **How I prioritize it.** Prospects near an A, B or C account first, so they land on a route I
  already drive.
- **How I improve it.** Score each prospect by distance to an anchor account, and grade the 127
  accounts with no grade yet.
- **Evidence.** 34 prospects landed from a Places search; 1 touched, none ordered.

### 9. Area prospecting on route days · improve the method
- **What it is.** Already in Newport Beach, finding the high-fit prospects nearby.
- **How it moves revenue.** Adds stops to a drive already paid for.
- **How I prioritize it.** Only to fill a day that is short of stops.
- **How I improve it.** Each planned day fills from 5 toward 8 stops with the nearest scored
  prospects.
- **Evidence.** Median 5 visits per field day.

### 10. New-client onboarding · improve the method
- **What it is.** Getting a new store from yes to a first order on its way.
- **How it moves revenue.** Today ordering works well only for existing clients. A clear path keeps a
  new yes from stalling.
- **How I prioritize it.** Every new account.
- **How I improve it.** One documented page: newaccounts@nutribiotic.com, the customer requirements,
  the $75 minimum, COD until credit is set then Net 30, and the new-store promotion for the first
  order.
- **Evidence.** No onboarding document exists in the repo.

### 11. Cold outbound email for awareness · improve the method
- **What it is.** High-volume email to the coldest leads, not waiting for replies. Two flavors:
  existing clients and potential clients.
- **How it moves revenue.** Indirectly: the subject line is read, so the next call lands warmer.
- **How I prioritize it.** After the work above, in batches.
- **How I improve it.** Two approved templates, each send logged as a touch, and replies linked to
  the email that earned them.
- **Evidence.** 18 drafts sent; 3 emails came back, none linked to a send. Measure to add: reply
  linkage on outbound drafts.

### 12. In-store staff training · improve the method
- **What it is.** Short trainings that teach a store's staff to recommend the product.
- **How it moves revenue.** Sell-through: the shelf empties faster and the reorder comes sooner.
  Armando named it, with follow-up, as the habit he repeats most.
- **How I prioritize it.** A pilot at 2 grade A accounts.
- **How I improve it.** Measure each pilot by a reorder within 60 days.
- **Evidence.** Not piloted yet (In-Store Program section 4).

### 13. Recording the visit · leave alone
Every visit is filed to the OS and HubSpot from one extractor, typed, spoken or recorded. Keep doing
it every time; the OS keeps improving on its own.

### 14. Decision-maker enrichment · leave alone
Head Hunter runs on its own and found a decision maker for 70 of 132 SDR accounts, up from 46.

### 15. Reports to the director of sales · leave alone
The daily and weekly field reports build themselves: visits and calls with summaries, the map,
purchases, accounts closed and opened. Only the Sunday hand-off is mine.

### 16. Biweekly expense report · leave alone
Photos of odometer readings and receipts file into the pay period's sheet. It does not move revenue;
keep it as light as it is.

### 17. Charging cards and sending invoices · leave alone
Billing is HQ's (accounting@nutribiotic.com). My part is relaying it accurately. Diligence over
speed.

## Test with the budget

### 18. TikTok Shop affiliates · test with the budget
- **What it is.** Paying creators a commission on every TikTok Shop sale of NutriBiotic, with sample sends to the ones who post.
- **How it moves revenue.** It is the one activity where return is read straight off the dashboard: sales per creator against commission and sample cost. NutriBiotic has a budget for it, so I can set a ROAS floor before spending and stop what misses it.
- **How I prioritize it.** Supplements are priced at 15 to 25 percent commission on the platform, and open collaboration runs lower than targeted collaboration. Start with a small creator list on targeted terms, then widen only what clears the floor. The floor counts commission, not only ad spend.
- **How I improve it.** One page per creator: sales, commission paid, samples sent, ROAS. A weekly read, and cut any creator under the floor after two weeks.
- **Evidence.** None yet. The OS holds no TikTok Shop sales, spend or creator data. Measure to add: spend, commission and sales per creator, in one table. Open before any spend: confirm NutriBiotic has a TikTok Shop seller account and that its supplement listings are approved. Not mine to confirm from here.

### 19. Reacher App for creator outreach · test with the budget
- **What it is.** The software that runs TikTok Shop affiliate outreach: finds creators, sends invites and messages, queues sample requests, tracks each creator's sales.
- **How it moves revenue.** It turns activity 18 from a manual chore into a measured channel. Its reporting is what makes ROAS per creator visible without a spreadsheet.
- **How I prioritize it.** Start on the Starter plan, $199 a month, and move to Pro, $599 a month, only if outreach volume or AI creator search is the limit. Pro adds AI creator search, automated follow-ups and unlimited daily messages.
- **How I improve it.** Run it for one month on one product line. Compare the month's creator sales to the plan cost plus commission.
- **Evidence.** Plan prices from reacherapp.com/pricing, read 2026-10-05. No trial or spend has started. Buying the plan is mine to approve, from the NutriBiotic budget.
