The Mixolydian mode

Why our songs float

By Stoke Club · September 28, 2026

Stoke Club is an indie surf rock band from Newport Beach, California. Our songs have a dreamy, floating feel. Most of that comes from one choice: we lean on the Mixolydian mode.

What Mixolydian is

Mixolydian is the major scale with one note changed. The seventh note drops a half step. That is the whole difference.

In a normal major scale the seventh note sits a half step under home and pulls hard toward it. It is the note that makes a melody feel like it has to resolve. Lower it by a half step and the pull disappears. The music stays bright, because the third is still major, but it stops insisting on coming home.

A MixolydianA B C# D E F# GG instead of G#
E MixolydianE F# G# A B C# DD instead of D#
G MixolydianG A B C D E FF instead of F#
D MixolydianD E F# G A B CC instead of C#

How to hear it

The fastest way to hear Mixolydian is with chords. Play a major chord, then play the major chord a whole step below it, then go back. E to D to E. G to F to G. That move, from the flat seventh chord back to home, is the sound. It feels open and a little sunlit, never tense.

A regular major key would bring you home through a dominant chord with a leading tone. Mixolydian brings you home sideways, through a chord a whole step away. Nothing snaps shut, so the song keeps drifting.

Why a surf rock band uses it

Surf music is about the feeling of a long summer afternoon. You do not want every phrase to land with a thud. Mixolydian keeps the major-key brightness and removes the urgency, which is exactly the mood we are after.

You can hear the move in both of our releases. Polaroid steps down from E to D in its verse turnaround. Crocodile Tears climbs from G to A in its chorus. We break both songs down in Polaroid and the Mixolydian mode and Crocodile Tears and the Mixolydian lift.

Try it yourself

Pick any major chord you like and alternate it with the major chord a whole step below. Strum slowly, let it ring, and hum over the top. If it sounds like the beach at golden hour, you have found it. Then put on Stoke Club on Spotify and listen for the same move.
