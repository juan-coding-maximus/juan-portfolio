The silence in Polaroid

Stopping before the chorus

By Stoke Club · September 28, 2026

Most songs build into the chorus. Polaroid does the opposite. Right before each chorus, the whole band stops.

The shape of the song

Polaroid runs intro, verse, prechorus, chorus, verse, prechorus, pause, chorus, solo. Both prechoruses are pure silence. The second time around, there is an extra pause stacked on top of it, so the final chorus arrives after the longest silence in the song.

Why silence works here

The verse ends on a chord progression that steps downhill, E to D to C# to B, a Mixolydian slide that never quite resolves. Instead of resolving it, we cut it off. The listener is left hanging on an open chord, and the chorus fills the gap.

A drum fill or a riser would tell you exactly when the chorus is coming. Silence does not. It turns the room quiet for a moment and makes the chorus land harder when it comes back.

Where it pays off

The last chorus is the payoff. Because of the extra pause, it comes in after the biggest gap, and then the solo takes over on the verse chords to close the song out.
