Crocodile Tears and the Mixolydian lift

G up to A

By Stoke Club · September 28, 2026

Crocodile Tears is the second single from Stoke Club, released July 3, 2026. It sits in G and it is slower than Polaroid: 93 beats per minute on the record, and we push it to 97 when we play it live.

The verse

The verse rocks between G and C twice, then moves to A and back to C. G to C is the most familiar move in the key of G. The A chord is the surprise. In the key of G you would expect A minor, and we play it major, which brightens the end of the phrase.

The chorus lift

The chorus plays G, A, C, four times over. The first step, G up to A, is two major chords a whole step apart. Hear A as home for a second and G is its flat seventh chord, the Mixolydian move running in reverse. It gives the chorus a lift without any strain, like being picked up by a wave instead of climbing a hill.

Then C opens it up, and the loop starts again. Nothing in the chorus resolves hard, so it can repeat four times and still feel like it is moving.

The ending

After the solo, the band plays the G, A, C pattern as hits, then lands on C over and over before one final G. After a whole song of floating, that last G is the only real landing.
