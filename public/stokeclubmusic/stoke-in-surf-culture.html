Stoke in surf culture

A feeling you pass on

By Stoke Club · September 28, 2026

Ask a surfer what stoke is and you'll probably get a story instead of a definition. It's the feeling after a good wave. It's also something you can hand to someone else. We're Stoke Club, a surf rock band from Newport Beach, and this is how the word works in the water and out of it.

The feeling after a wave

Stoke starts with a ride. You paddle, you catch it, you stand, and for a few seconds everything lines up. When you come up, you are grinning. That's stoke. The Surf Education Academy describes it simply: to be stoked means to be fired up in the best way possible. It's joy. It's energy.

It doesn't need a big wave. A first wave on a foam board counts. So does a clean little peak on a Tuesday morning before work. One surf historian, quoted by The Wipeout Weekly, called it that sense of challenge, adventure, exhilaration, and god-kissed bliss that imbues the surfing experience.

Sharing the stoke

The part of surf culture we like most is that stoke isn't meant to be kept. You see it in the lineup. Someone gets a good one and the people paddling back out hoot for them. A regular tells a beginner where to sit. Friends talk through every wave in the parking lot afterward.

The Surf Education Academy puts it simply: stoke grows when it's shared. Cheering for someone else's wave costs you nothing, and it doubles the feeling. That's why share the stoke became a phrase at all. It treats a good mood as something with value that you pass along.

Stoke as community

Once the word leaves the water, it becomes a way of treating people. Surfers talk about stoke for a new job, an exam, a friend's good news. You also hear it in places with no ocean at all. The Inertia wrote about surfers on Lake Michigan and their unsalted stoke. The waves are different, the feeling is the same.

That's what it really means to us. Stoke is a feeling, and it's also a habit of giving that feeling to the people around you. The surfer who hoots for your wave is doing both.

Surf music

Surf music has always tried to bottle this. The Beach Boys named an instrumental Stoked in 1963, and surf bands have been chasing that afternoon feeling since. We try to do the same with open, floating chords, the kind we break down in The Mixolydian mode.

The easiest way to share stoke with us is to be in the room. We play shows, and you can sign up to get invited on the Stoke Club shows page. Before that, put on Polaroid. For the history of the word itself, read What stoke means.
